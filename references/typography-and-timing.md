# Typography and lyric timing

## Timing layers

Separate:

- line timing — lyric line becomes relevant;
- phrase timing — sung phrase begins and ends;
- word or character timing — emphasized units appear;
- shot timing — visual cut or camera change;
- translation timing — readable support, usually tied to the phrase rather than every character.

LRC line starts are a starting point. If the singer finishes early, end or resolve the lyric at the vocal ending instead of stretching animation through an instrumental.

## Japanese lyric motion

Use expressive type selectively. The motion should reflect syntax, stress, or image:

- repeated words can echo spatially;
- a delayed noun can be revealed separately;
- a negated word can be crossed, displaced, erased, or replaced once;
- fast lyrics can enter in clusters rather than one slow line;
- an emotional apology or confession can grow, fracture, crowd, or momentarily overwhelm the scene.

Do not repeat the same gimmick until it loses meaning.

## Chinese translation

Unless directed otherwise:

- use a common Chinese sans-serif or readable song-appropriate serif;
- keep natural character spacing;
- place it in a stable lower-left or lower safe area;
- use no opaque box unless contrast truly requires one;
- size it below the main Japanese lyric while preserving mobile readability;
- avoid overlap with important faces, hands, and props.

Use a subtle outline, shadow, local gradient, or composition-aware dark/light colour for contrast. Never assume white text works on every frame.

## Visual integration

Lyrics should share the scene's perspective, rhythm, palette, and edge treatment. A revision must inherit the established type family, weight, outline, placement logic, and entrance language. A corrected line that looks like a pasted subtitle is still a failed correction.

## Instrumentals

Do not show countdowns, dummy lyrics, or explanatory labels unless part of the concept. Let images, ambient type fragments, credits, or intentional silence carry the section.

## Checks

At normal playback speed verify:

- first visible frame is not late;
- words do not appear after they are sung;
- all characters fit within title-safe bounds;
- two simultaneous text systems remain legible;
- text clears before the next incompatible composition;
- translations do not flash too briefly to read;
- repeated choruses evolve without losing recognition.
