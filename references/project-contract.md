# Project contract and persistence

Create a small written source of truth at the start of a long PV project. Suggested files:

- `PROJECT.md`: audio, lyric, translation, duration, output formats, credits, approval state.
- `CONCEPT.md`: thesis, story or atmosphere arc, palette, motifs, forbidden directions.
- `CHARACTER.md`: official visual facts and asset-by-asset consistency checklist.
- `STORYBOARD.md`: timed shot list and instrumental plan.
- `PROGRESS.md`: accepted, rejected, unresolved, generated assets, renders, and next action.

## Distinguish reference roles

Label every image or video as one of:

- **identity reference** — authoritative character, costume, prop, or logo design;
- **style reference** — line, colour, compositing, texture, or typography;
- **motion reference** — timing, camera, transition, or lyric entrance;
- **mood reference** — emotion or narrative association only;
- **do-not-use asset** — may be discussed but must not appear in output.

Never infer that a mood reference is permission to copy its design or include it in the PV.

## Feedback ledger

For each revision, record:

- what the user accepted;
- what they rejected and why;
- whether the fix changes an asset, scene logic, typography, timing, or delivery only;
- which previous build remains the best baseline;
- which files must remain untouched.

When feedback conflicts, official art and the latest explicit direction win. If that would invalidate a large approved portion, explain the conflict before rebuilding.

## Preservation

Until explicit final approval:

- do not delete source art, prompts, timelines, older renders, or review sheets;
- do not replace the only copy of an accepted asset;
- version outputs and keep a reproducible entry point for each meaningful preview;
- save progress before long renders or usage limits.

After final approval, delete only the scope the user clearly names and report what remains recoverable.
