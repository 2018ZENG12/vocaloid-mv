# Character and asset fidelity

## Character bible

Extract only visible, testable facts from official references:

- face width and chin shape;
- eye shape, pupil value, spacing, and eyebrow angle;
- hair silhouette, volume, parting, cowlicks, and accessories;
- garment layers, closures, sleeve and hem shapes, footwear, and age-specific items;
- nonhuman ornaments: number, lobe count, attachment or floating gap, side, scale, and orientation;
- props: proportions, seams, fill level, broken or sealed ends, and grip position;
- palette and line quality.

Do not compensate for uncertainty by adding generic anime detail. More polish can make a character less faithful.

## Generation sequence

1. Make a neutral master candidate before action poses.
2. Compare it side by side with official art at similar scale.
3. Fix identity errors before generating a batch.
4. Use the approved master and official reference for each later pose.
5. Generate backgrounds separately when character integration is unreliable.
6. Composite with matching line weight, palette, grain, shadow logic, and perspective.

For complex objects, create a small prop reference first. Confirm shape and handling before placing it into full scenes.

## Asset matrix

Map each asset to one or more storyboard shots and mark:

- characters present;
- pose and camera distance;
- location and time of day;
- required negative space for lyrics;
- foreground, midground, and background separation;
- whether animation needs alternate pose, cutout layers, or a clean plate;
- reuse limit and planned transformation.

If one image appears repeatedly, its later use must change meaning through context, not only crop or colour.

## Full-resolution rejection checks

Reject or repair assets with:

- inconsistent eyes, face age, hair volume, or silhouette;
- missing, duplicated, attached, or wrongly oriented ornaments;
- implausible neck, shoulder, hand, leg, or clothing construction;
- props with wrong ends, fill, grip, scale, or count;
- unexplained black lines crossing the face;
- characters cut by the frame or mask in a way that looks accidental;
- excessive rendering polish compared with the official line-art style;
- backgrounds whose perspective or lighting makes the character look pasted on.

When an image cannot support the required action, change the shot or generate a suitable pose. Do not hide anatomy problems with effects.
