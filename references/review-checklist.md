# PV review checklist

Review the encoded result, not only the live canvas or editor timeline.

## Story and imagery

- Does every section have a visible purpose?
- Are concrete places, props, actions, or consequences present often enough to anchor the film?
- Do abstract-only shots accumulate into a generic design reel?
- Does the instrumental develop rather than fill time?
- Is reused material returning with new meaning?

## Character and assets

- Does the character read correctly at first glance?
- Are face, eyes, hair, costume, ornaments, and props faithful to the accepted reference?
- Are all limbs and floating parts complete and uncropped?
- Does the character belong to the background in perspective, texture, palette, and lighting?
- Is any pose anatomically strange at full resolution?

## Motion and editing

- Are cuts and major changes aligned with audible structure?
- Does the camera reveal or express something, or merely move?
- Are adjacent shots using the same zoom, shake, or pan?
- Does any still character appear to slide like a sticker?
- Is a complex action being faked beyond what the source art can support?
- Is there enough stillness to make bursts feel strong?

## Typography

- Are main lyrics readable against every background?
- Is translation in a common readable font with natural spacing?
- Are lines visually integrated rather than pasted on?
- Are vocals and lyric exits aligned?
- Are instrumentals free of countdowns and placeholders?
- Are all lines complete and within safe margins?

## Technical delivery

- correct duration, resolution, frame rate, aspect ratio, and audio;
- no missing or duplicated frames at segment joins;
- no nondeterministic flicker;
- final file decodes from start to finish;
- encoded contact sheet checked across the entire runtime;
- opening, chorus, instrumental, climax, and ending watched at normal speed;
- credits accurate;
- previous accepted version retained until final approval.

## Revision diagnosis

Translate vague feedback into the correct layer:

- “单调 / 重复” → shot plan, asset budget, or motion grammar;
- “像 PPT / 贴图” → depth, interaction, compositing, or scene action;
- “不像角色” → character bible and asset regeneration;
- “没卡点” → phrase timing, not more effects;
- “太抽象” → add concrete narrative anchors;
- “动作怪” → source pose unsuitable; replace the action language;
- “文字看不清” → contrast and composition, not necessarily a larger box.

Do not solve a structural complaint with a cosmetic effect.
